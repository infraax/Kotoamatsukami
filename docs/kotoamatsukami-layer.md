# KOTOAMATSUKAMI Layer — Bounded Decision Intervention

**KOTO is an engineering metaphor for improving decisions through transparent, authorized interventions.** The fictional technique operates covertly; this design deliberately does not replicate covert mind control.

## When it activates

Shisui's diagnosis indicates that a decision is materially blocked by a resolvable evidence gap, conflicting objectives, a mismatch between risk tolerance and the plan, or an outdated assumption. If the issue is simply lack of capability or a policy prohibition, KOTO cannot turn that into permission.

## Intervention menu

1. **Evidence addition:** provide a missing benchmark, source, test, or observation.
2. **Transparent reframing:** restate trade-offs against the decision owner's documented criteria.
3. **Option expansion:** offer a lower-risk, reversible, or staged alternative.
4. **Negotiated commitment:** ask the authorized owner to agree to a time-bounded experiment or explicit criterion.
5. **Policy update request:** route a proposed change through its actual approval process. Agents may request but not self-approve it.

## KOTO protocol

1. State the decision and the observed blocker.
2. List evidence and plausible alternative explanations.
3. Select the minimum effective intervention.
4. Check authorization, explainability, reversibility, privacy, and proportionality.
5. Present the intervention and its rationale to the proper decision owner.
6. Observe what changes; do not assume a changed answer means a better outcome.
7. Ask the independent verifier to evaluate goal progress, side effects, and stability.
8. Record expiry and rollback/compensation path where an actual state change occurred.

## Hard rejection conditions

Reject intervention if it depends on deception about evidence, hidden influence, fabricated urgency, impersonation, coercion, use of irrelevant private data, or bypassing a valid refusal/approval gate. When an intervention is unauthorized or unclear, provide ordinary advice, request clarification, or stop.

## Outcome metrics

- decision quality against predefined criteria;
- time to resolve the evidence gap;
- reversibility and side-effect cost;
- rate of interventions rejected by policy;
- whether the decision remains sound when new evidence arrives;
- whether the action was freely and explicitly approved when approval was required.
